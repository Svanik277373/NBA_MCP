# League Notes — Attacking Switch-Heavy Defenses

Switch-everything defensive schemes have become the default counter to
modern spread pick-and-roll offenses, since they take away the paint-touch
advantage a roll man usually creates without requiring a help defender to
rotate from the weak side. The tradeoff is mismatch hunting: once a switch
happens, the offense can attack whichever mismatch is most favorable —
typically a smaller guard switched onto a big, or a slower big switched
onto a ball-handling wing.

Teams that beat switching defenses consistently share a few traits: a
primary shot creator strong enough to punish a smaller defender in the post
or with a face-up, quick ball reversal to hunt the mismatch on the weak
side before the defense can re-switch, and screening actions designed to
target a specific defender (hunting a slow big into space) rather than
generic ball-screens.

Teams that struggle against switching share the opposite profile: offenses
that rely heavily on a roll man's paint touches (since switching removes
that advantage entirely), or shot creators who aren't strong enough to
punish a smaller defender in isolation once switched onto them. These teams
tend to see their offensive rating drop several points against high-switch
opponents relative to their season average.
