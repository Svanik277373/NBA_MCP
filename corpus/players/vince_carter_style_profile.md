# Vince Carter — Style Profile (1999-2001 Toronto)

Carter's early Toronto seasons were defined by explosive downhill
athleticism paired with a perimeter shot that was still rounding into
form. He relied on top-tier vertical burst and open-court speed to get
to the rim and finish above the defense before opponents fully
game-planned for his coast-to-coast ability, and he was at his most
efficient attacking a defense that hadn't yet set.

His half-court shot creation was still developing relative to his athletic
tools — the pull-up jumper was inconsistent possession-to-possession,
which meant defenses could sag off him early in the shot clock and dare
him to shoot before he punished them off the dribble. As his catch-and-shoot
and midrange efficiency improved in following seasons, defenses could no
longer play him that way, and his overall scoring efficiency rose
accordingly even as his usage stayed high.

He was an above-average team defender in this stretch on effort and
length alone, without being a plus point-of-attack defender — a similar
profile to other high-usage perimeter scorers whose defensive engagement
tracked closely with their offensive workload that night.
