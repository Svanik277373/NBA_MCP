# Tracy McGrady — Style Profile (2001-2004 Orlando/Houston)

McGrady's peak seasons were built on a shot-creation profile centered on
live-dribble pull-up threes and downhill drives created off his own
handle. He carried enormous offensive usage on teams that ran their entire
half-court offense through him, generating his own looks with minimal
off-ball movement or screening help — nearly everything came from
isolation or simple ball-screen actions where he was the primary read.

Physically, he combined length, a high release point, and enough burst to
get downhill against a set defense, which made him difficult to
game-plan for one-on-one. His efficiency was volume-dependent: on nights
where he settled for the pull-up three early in possessions, his true
shooting suffered, but when he used the threat of the drive to draw
help before kicking or finishing, his efficiency numbers were elite for
his usage rate.

Defensively he was more engaged than his reputation suggested in these
seasons, using his length to contest without needing to be a great
on-ball point-of-attack defender, but he was miscast as a full-time
point-of-attack stopper given the offensive workload he carried.
