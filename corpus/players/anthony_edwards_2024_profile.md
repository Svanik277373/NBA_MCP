# Anthony Edwards — 2024-25 Scouting Profile

Edwards has expanded his shot diet to more pull-up threes off the dribble
this season, a deliberate shift after Minnesota's playoff run exposed how
much defenses were willing to concede the catch-and-shoot look. Opponents
now chase him hard over screens rather than conceding the three, and his
catch-and-shoot efficiency has dipped as a direct result — he is taking a
tougher shot more often by design, not by accident.

The tradeoff shows up clearly in his shooting splits: catch-and-shoot three
point percentage is down from last season, while pull-up three volume is up
significantly. Defenses have adjusted by sending a second defender to
tag him on live-dribble situations, which has modestly increased his
turnover rate but also opened driving lanes for his teammates when he draws
the extra attention.

Physically, Edwards remains one of the most explosive downhill athletes in
the league. He gets to the rim at a high rate and finishes through contact,
though his free-throw rate has not kept pace with his drive frequency —
officiating has been inconsistent on whether contact initiated by the
ball-handler draws a whistle.

Defensively, Edwards is inconsistent possession-to-possession. He has the
physical tools (length, lateral quickness, closing speed) to be a plus
point-of-attack defender, but his effort dips noticeably in the regular
season when he is carrying a heavy offensive workload. In switch-heavy
schemes, he is capable of guarding up to power forwards for stretches
without being a liability.

Overall efficiency (true shooting %) has come down modestly year over year.
The primary driver is shot selection — a higher share of tougher,
self-created looks — rather than a decline in raw shooting touch. Scouts
should expect the efficiency number to stabilize or improve as he refines
touch on the pull-up three, a shot he has taken at increasing volume each of
the last three seasons.
