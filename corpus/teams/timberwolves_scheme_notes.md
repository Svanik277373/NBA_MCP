# Minnesota Timberwolves — Offensive Scheme Notes

Minnesota's half-court offense has grown more isolation-heavy in clutch
minutes this season, a shift from the more egalitarian ball movement they
featured in the regular rotation. That trend correlates with a leaguewide
drop in assisted-3PT rate for their wings in the final five minutes of close
games — shooters are getting fewer catch-and-shoot looks late and more
possessions are ending in a contested, self-created shot.

Against switch-heavy defenses specifically, Minnesota's offense has been
uneven. When Edwards is attacking a switch, the offense is efficient — his
combination of strength and ballhandling lets him bully smaller defenders
or blow by bigger ones. But when the ball moves away from him against a
switching scheme, the team's other shot creators (particularly the bigs)
struggle to generate quality looks, and possessions often stall into a
late-clock isolation. Teams that switch everything and are willing to live
with Edwards attacking a mismatch, while taking the ball out of his hands
during ball reversals, have had success slowing Minnesota's offense down.

Gobert's screening gravity is a swing factor: when he sets a hard, live
screen, the following switch is more disruptive to the defense's rotations
because it forces a big to hedge or show, opening driving lanes. When he
screens passively (common late in games when he's used more as a floor
spacer near the dunker spot), switch defenses have an easier time staying
attached without conceding an advantage.
